144. INTRODUCTION

145. TDC Ghana Ltd. formerly known as the Tema Development Company (TDC) was set up in 1952 by an Act of Parliament with the sole responsibility to plan and develop about 63 square miles of public land for various land uses and also manage the Township that had been created to provide accommodation to those that would engage in economic operations in and around the Tema Township. The Company was given an initial 125-year lease in 1955 to manage this land as the Tema Acquisition Area. The lease has been subsequently renewed for another 125 years effective 1st January 2024.

146. There are seven departments in the Company namely, Estates, Administration, Finance, Audit, Development, Corporate Planning and Communication and Legal. The supporting units include the Procurement, Marketing, Development Control and MIS units. These departments and units work collaboratively to enable the Company to accomplish its set targets and aims. The Estates Department is undoubtedly the reception for most of the Company’s lessees if not all and it is the first point of call in the Company by clients.

147. The Department is the estates management wing of the Company. Its primary responsibility/function is the supervision and control of the Company’s landed interests towards ensuring an optimum realization of returns to the estates. These estates/landed interests include residential, commercial, industrial, institutional, recreational and agricultural lands and a portfolio of rental housing units, shops and offices within and restricted to the Tema Township.

164. 1.1 OVERVIEW

165. These stations are the first point of call in the department for lessees and clients whenever they want to make a follow up on their applications, make some enquiries and to seek clarification on other related land and property issues.

168. 1.3 DUTIES OF THE SECRETARIAL SECTION

169. Duties at the Secretarial Section are divided into two.

170. The first involves providing secretarial duties for the Head of Estates at Block ‘B’, Room 230 and these include;

171. Receiving Incoming Files- Files coming from various departments to the HOE are received at his reception. The details of the property file are recorded in their various books. There are specific incoming notebooks in which Regularization & Kpone files are recorded. Any other file is recorded in the General notebook. Details captured include the Property No, Property Name, Who the file is coming from; the Date the file was brought in and the Action/Comment in the file.

172. Receiving Incoming Letter – Incoming Letters referred to the Head of Estates are also received and recorded in the Letters Book. Details captured include the Subject of the letter, the date the letter was received at the HOE’s secretarial and the name of the Applicant.

173. Dispatching Outgoing Letters and Files within the Department - Files and Letters that have been worked on by the HOE are dispatch ed to the Estates Registry to be given to the various EM/EO after it has been recorded in the appropriate books. Details captured include the date it was referred by the HOE and the Officer to whom it has been referred.

174. Receiving Visitors – All visitors who call at HOE’s outfit are welcomed by the secretariat. The secretariat ought to find out if the visitor has an appointment with the

175. HOE or the purpose for the visit. If it is an issue that is within the jurisdiction of the secretariat, they attend to the visitor or direct the visitor to the appropriate quarters for assistance.

176. Visitors also sometimes come to pick up Estate Transfer Forms, House Ownership Scheme Forms and Rental Unit Form after they have paid for them and have been issued with a valid receipt.

177. The date of purchase, the receipt number, the name on the receipt, the property number and the EM/EO who permitted the purchase are all captured in the Forms Purchase book for future reference.

178. Tracing of Incoming and Outgoing Letters–The HOE’s secretariat also assist visitors, Supervisors and Estates Assistants to trace the movement of files and letters from the various incoming and outgoing books to know their specific locations.

179. Receiving and making of Telephone calls - Calls are received and also made for HOE by using the internal telephone directorate as and when the need arises.

180. Maintaining the HOE’s Office - Secretaries are responsible for maintaining the HOE’s office by keeping the place tidy always, arrange the place for internal /external meetings and also serving tea and snacks sometimes.

181. Typing– The HOE’s secretariat type drafts, memos, letters, and quarterly reports for the HOE, EM, EO and the EAs. They also compile and type the list of applications for the Property Allocation Committee for consideration according to the various land uses i.e. Residential, Commercial, Institutional, Light Industrial and Heavy Industrial. They sometimes take minutes of meetings and draft letters for approval.

182. Regular Updates of Processes to Clients -	 the secretariat regularly updates all clients and lessees of the status of the requests which are in the department. This is done by sending text messages to the applicants.

198. CHAPTER 2

199. RECORDS SECTION

200. 2.1. OVERVIEW;

201. The work of this section cannot be underestimated because it is tasked with maintaining and managing all the records of the properties disposed by the Company and keeping an eagle’s eye on the conditions of lease.

202. 2.2. DUTIES

203. 2.2.1. UPDATING OF RECORDS:

204. Estate Registers:

205. This involves building records on new allocations in our Residential, Agricultural, Commercial, Institutional and Industrial plots. It usually includes recording the right of entry dates or date of tenancy, lease term, ground rent payable, acreage/plot size and lessee(s) name and address.

206. Ledger Cards:

207. There are also the H.O.S. ledger cards where records of the various houses in the                Communities in Tema are recorded.  In these cards we have a section for rental which indicates that the tenant has not purchased the property yet.  We have the name, the house number and the date on which the tenant(s) rented the property.

208. The other section is the H.O.S which basically means the lessee has purchased the property. With this, the lessee’s names, date on which property was purchased and the amount paid are recorded into the ledger.  The House type is also noted.

209. Then there is the remarks column where recordings like tenancy termination on grounds of death, non-usage of plot, non-payment of ground rent are noted.

210. 2.2.2 AMENDMENT OF RECORDS:

211. Change Of Address:

212. Lessees may also wish to change their addresses from time to time. It is the duty of the records section to amend to reflect these new addresses. At times, additional name(s) are added to existing ones upon lessee’s request.  Apart from the Supervisor stating in the file to change address, a letter with lessee’s new address and a statutory declaration may support this change in the property file.

213. Transfer Of Property:

214. Amendment after a transfer is one of the frequent exercises performed at the Records section.  Lessees transfer and assign property occasionally and whenever this occurs; it is the duty of the records section to change existing records to reflect the new ones in our registers and ledgers. For example, whenever a transfer of an interest in land is completed, a copy of the Company’s Transfer Declaration form completed by both transferor and transferee would be seen in the file showing voluntary vacation of tenancy of clients. The House/Plot/Shop number, date on which transfer was effected, address of new lessee etc. is also made available. However, if the property is on rental changing to H.O.S, a form showing the name(s) of tenant(s) changing to H.O.S, is put in file. This is known as the House Ownership Scheme Forms.

215. The house type and amount for which property was bought is also indicated on this form. All these give one the clue and guidelines as to how transfers are done in both the ledgers and registers at records.

216. 2.2.3 NOTIFICATION LETTERS TO AGENCIES / OTHER INSTITUTIONS:

217. The section writes to various agencies like Ghana Water Company, Electricity Company of Ghana and other stakeholders when transfers are done.  This is mainly to inform these agencies that, new lessees have taken over a property and that their records should be updated likewise in order to send bills to the right lessee(s). Special forms are designed for this purpose.

218. 2.2.4 DEVELOPMENT APPLICATION AND VERIFICATION:

219. The Records section also works on Building Permit Applications, Verification of Ownership and indebtedness form. They are usually referred to the Records section by the Building Inspectorate Supervisor of the Development Department for confirmation of Ownership and Right of Entry/Date of tenancy of properties and land use.  If for any reason names do not tally with that of Revenue and Development, the Supervisor of Records and Head of Estates would not append their signatures for clients to get a permit until all three departments confirm same information for client.

220. 2.2.5 INVITATION LETTERS:

221. The section also prepares invitation letters to tenants.  This is mainly written to multiple tenants who submitted application for extension through the Development section. The section invites tenants to the Records Supervisor’s office with their letters to settle their differences and also confirm signatories to the Statutory Declaration submitted as consents.  For example, if tenant(s) wants to expand their homes, sample questions like the following are asked by the Supervisor of Records:

222. Who proposed the idea for the extension?

223. If the other tenants are aware of the proposal/application.

224. If legal tenants are absent, the status/capacity of the Representative to stand in for legal tenant.

225. Why the refusal for a Co-tenant to give his consent if any.

226. Any other questions and advice as the interview may determine.

227. The form or invitation consist of the lessee’s name and address, and a column showing clients the room number of Supervisor and time to report. After preparation of letters, they are stamped for clients to see how official or authentic it is. Lessees are also asked to bring along these letters when coming for interview with the Supervisor.

252. 3.3. DRAFTING OF RESPONSE LETTERS

253. There are instances where peculiar responses need to be given to clients’ requests. These responses have no template hence a draft is prepared by the EM/EO/EA for approval per the minutes stated on the letter/in the file before a final letter is prepared for signing by the HOE.

254. 3.4. INSPECTION OF LAND/LANDED PROPERTY

255. This is done for the purpose of writing a site report. The current development on the site is usually captured. Location, neighborhood details and pictures of the site are taken to ensure a comprehensive site report.

256. 3.5. SEARCH APPLICATION

257. A search request is submitted by the applicant/lessee.

258. The lessee is expected to have settled all ground rent arrears before submitting the application.

259. A search fee is charged depending on the acreage/plot size.

260. A search report is prepared and referred to HOE for signing and subsequent dispatch.

261. 3.6. CHANGE OF ADDRESS

262. Lessee submits request for change of address.

263. Lessee is expected to have settled all Ground Rent arrears.

264. If applicable, the EO/EM checks for supporting documents (such as statutory Declaration, valid Identification Card, Letters of Administration, Power of Attorney, Court Order etc.) as well as their validity/ authenticity.

265. EO refers file to Revenue & Estates records to amend records if requirements are met.

266. An amendment letter informing lessee of successful change of address is prepared and EO/EM refers it to HOE for signing before dispatch.

267. 3.7. PROCESSING OF CERTIFIED TRUE COPIES

268. Lessee submits an application for Certified True Copy of documents needed.

269. Lessee is expected to have settled all Ground Rent arrears.

270. EO/EM calculates administrative fee chargeable, and lessee is informed accordingly to pay.

271. After payment, documents are prepared and certified within the Estates department or EO/EM refers file to HOD/HOL through HOE for a copy of the requested document.

272. A Certified True Copy of documents requested   together with a cover letter is prepared and EO/EM refers file to HOE for signing before dispatch.

273. 3.8. JOINT OWNERSHIP/ADDITION OF NAME

274. Lessee submits request together with a Statutory Declaration.

275. Lessee is expected to have settled all ground rent arrears.

276. If applicable, EM/EO checks Letters of Administration, Power of Attorney, Court Order etc. for its authenticity.

277. EM/EO checks if there is a lease on the property.

278. If there is a lease, EM/EO refers file to HOL through HOE for cadastral plans. After cadastral plans have been prepared and the requisite fees paid for, file is referred to HOL through HOE to prepare a deed of variation. After registration at Lands Commission, a copy is submitted for detachment at Legal and records amended at Revenue & Estates Records.

279. EM/EO refers file to HOE for approval.

280. If approved, a demand letter requesting for an Administrative Fee from lessee is prepared and signed by HOE before dispatch.

281. After payment, EM/EO refers file to Revenue & Estates Records for amendment.

282. A Notification/Amendment Letter signed by the HOE is sent to parties informing them of a successful completion of process.

283. 3.9. TRANSFER / PORTION TRANSFER OF PLOT

284. Lessee/Applicant submits a request for a transfer.

285. Request together with the property file is referred to HOL for further processing.

286. Where the request has met all the requirements, HOL refers to HOE for the department to compute the transfer fee payable.

287. EM/EO calculates transfer fee payable refers same to HOE for further processing.

288. HOE approves the transfer fee and refers the file with all the transfer documents to MD for approval and further processing at the Legal Department.

289. If the transfer process is complete, the file is returned to the EM/EO through HOE for records amendment.

290. Records are amended at both Revenue & Estate Records.

291. A transfer completion/notification letter prepared by the EA and referred by the EM/EO is signed by HOE and sent to both transferor/transferees informing them about transfer completion.

292. File is referred to HOD by HOE for update of records and New site plans at the planning section.

293. A notification letter together with the new site plan is signed by HOE and dispatched to new lessee.

294. 3.10. ASSIGNMENT

295. An assignment process is only applicable if there is a registered lease on the property.

296. Lessee/applicant submits a request for consent to assign together with a draft deed of assignment.

297. Lessee is expected to have paid all ground rent arrears and should have substantially developed plot.

298. Request together with the property file is referred to HOL for further processing.

299. File is referred to HOD through HOL for cadastral plans in the name of assignee(s).

300. File is returned to EM/EO through HOE after cadastral plan is prepared for calculation of both consent fee & cadastral plan fees payable.

301. A letter indicating fees payable is prepared, signed by the MD and sent to both assignor & assignee for subsequent payment.

302. Upon payment, file is referred to HOL through HOE to continue assignment process

303. After registration of the deed of assignment at the Lands Commission, a copy of the document is submitted for detachment at the Legal Department.

304. File is referred back to EM/EO through HOE for records amendment.

305. EM/EO refers file to Revenue & Estates Records for amendment.

306. An assignment completion/notification letter informing both Assignor &Assignee of assignment completion is prepared and sent to HOE to be signed before dispatch.

307. File is referred to HOD by HOE for update of records and New site plans at the planning section.

308. A notification letter together with the new site plan is signed by HOE and dispatched to new lessee.

309. 3.11. CONSENT TO MORTGAGE / MORTGAGE IN PRINCIPLE

310. Lessee submits request for consent to mortgage together with a draft deed of mortgage.

311. If it’s from a Bank, a written consent from the Lessee is to be attached.

312. Lessee is expected to have settled all ground rent arrears.

313. File is referred to HOL through HOE for further processing.

314. After consent to Mortgage letter has been processed for the Lessee and file is returned to the Estates Department, EM/EO refers file to Records section to update records to reflect mortgage on property.

315. 3.12. PREPARATION OF LEASE DOCUMENTS

316. Our Offer Letters issued out to Lessees indicate that the Company will prepare a lease document for them upon their request & after they have substantially developed their plot. Determination of substantial development is at the discretion of the EO/EM. However, all developments must be done with the appropriate permitting documents.

317. As a requirement of the LI 1444, all such plans to be registered at the Lands Commission must be a cadastral plan.

318. Process

319. Lessee/Applicant submits an application requesting for a lease document.

320. Lessee is expected to have settled all ground rent arrears and should have substantially developed plot.

321. Where applicable, file is referred to BIS to confirm issuance of building Permit.

322. EO/EM refers file to HOL through HOE for vetting and further processing of application.

323. HOL refers file to HOD for preparation of cadastral plans in the name of lessee(s).

324. File is returned back to EO/EM through HOE after cadastral plans have been prepared preparation of a cadastral Invoice. An approved chart is used in the calculation of the fees and is dependent on the size/acreage of the plot.

325. The various fees charged are as follows:

326. Plan Approval Fee

327. Parcel Plan Fee

328. TDC Cadastral Plan Fee

329. Presentation and Communication Fees.

330. Lease Preparation fee

331. The letter signed by MD is dispatched to the lessee to inform him/her of the fees payable.

332. Upon payment of the fees, the receipts are attached to file and lease request forms are prepared. Lease Request forms are to advise our Legal Department on information on the plot for the preparation of the lease.

333. The form spells out the following:

334. Name and Address of the lessee

335. Size/Acreage of the plot as indicated on the Cadastral plan and the plot number

336. The current passing annual G/R

337. The use of the plot

338. The term of the lease and date of tenancy

339. The receipt numbers in the case of an H.O.S property

340. EO/EM refers file to HOL through the HOE for further processing of lease documents.

341. After registration of the lease documents at the Lands Commission, lessee submits a copy for detachment at the Legal department.

342. File is referred to EM/EO through HOE for records update after detachment.

701. CHAPTER 8

702. REGULARIZATION

703. Land Regularization is generally viewed as a process whereby the appropriate authority puts in measures and interventions on encroached lands and illegal developments to provide infrastructural improvements and to recognize ownership titles or other occupancy rights all in an effort to improve the livelihoods of the residents.

704. Large tracks of lands within the TDC Ghana Ltd’s Acquisition Area have been illegally occupied or developed by people without any proper tenancies or development permits.  These situations have arisen through various unauthorized actions by occupants as follows:

705. Lands which were officially offered to tenants as farmlands but over the years the lessees have illegally changed the user to residential without due process of planning approval by TDC;

706. Encroachment on various types of lands not officially offered to the occupants.

707. The effect of these actions has been the spread of unplanned developments in several areas.  Additionally, it has made it difficult for TDC to keep up-to-date data on these occupied lands, thus making the Company lose potential revenue which could have accrued from Land Management Fees, Ground Rent, and Development Permit Fees etc.

708. 8.2. PURPOSE:

709. The purpose of the Regularization is twofold:

710. To arrest and regulate the spread of unplanned developments by imposing a ’tailored’ plan on the target areas;

711. To identify tenants and collect and update data on them for revenue collection.

712. It should be noted that regularization of tenancies will only be for lands which satisfy the approval criteria of the new plan layout, and does not automatically grant approval or permit for the building or development.  The tenant must necessarily apply afterwards for a development permit. The Regularization schedule is under the Housing section and is managed by the Estates Manager (Housing&Lands), Estates Officer (Regularization) and the Estates Assistants (Regularization).

730. Carryout a proper survey documentation of lands in areas where lands have been illegally developed, identify plot boundaries and all physical features on the land.

731. Prepare New Plan Layout for the area or revise the existing plan layout within the context of the   unapproved developments and number all identified.

739. Identify developed plots which do not satisfy the approval criteria of the new plan layout and refer these to Development Control Unit for necessary action.

751. The designated ‘Regularization Office’ receives the required documents for regularization and confirms the plot number with the site plan at the Planning Section.

752. Applicants are interviewed and necessary checks are done to confirm ownership of the land after which applicant is approved to purchase and complete a TDC Regularization Application form.

753. A file is opened with the completed application form and attached documents and referred to the Revenue & Estates Records section for confirmation of any available information on plot.

754. File is referred for vetting of application by the Regularization Committee and a report submitted for approval and further processing.

755. File is referred to HOD for preparation of Site Plan.

756. Estates Department prepares a Proposal Letter indicating fees payable and refers to the MD for sign after which proposal letter is dispatched at the Registry.

757. Applicant submits an acceptance letter after full payment of Land Regularization Fees and file is referred for confirmation of payment by Revenue Accounts Supervisor.

758. After confirmation of payment, the Offer and Right of Entry Letters are prepared for signing by MD and HOE respectively after which documents are dispatched to applicant by the Registry.

759. PROCEDURE (2) – INDIRECT APPROACH

760. Submission of an application for Regularization at the Registry.

761. MD refers application to Regularization Committee to confirm location, investigate/vet application and submit a report for approval and further processing.

762. Application is referred to the Estates department after approval of Committee’s report to be placed on file.

763. A Waiting file (WT) is opened and applicants are invited for an interview and to submit any additional documents where applicable.

764. The Waiting file is referred to HOD for provision of plot number and preparation of Site Plan.

765. File is referred to the Estates Department for further processing after preparation of site plan.

766. Applicant is invited to purchase and complete a TDC Regularization Application form.

767. A Proposal Letter indicating fees payable is prepared and referred to the MD for sign after which proposal letter is dispatched at the Registry.

768. Applicant submits an acceptance letter after full payment of required fees and file is referred for confirmation of payment by Revenue Accounts Supervisor.

769. After confirmation, the Offer and Right of Entry Letters are prepared for signing by the MD and HOE respectively after which documents are dispatched to applicant by the Registry.
